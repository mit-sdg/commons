# Distinguish readable model replies from complete task results

## Current behavior

The sorting reader can accept a reply that omits waiting cards or repeats a placement. Participant and sample readers discard entries they cannot read; their acceptance alone does not establish complete box coverage or fidelity to offered choices. The pile-summary reader checks recognizable content and identity, but not the prompt's twelve-word limit. These are reader properties found during the direct model-context evaluation; they are not claims that the evaluated live replies exhibited every failure.

Relay dependency design, preview evidence and hosting guidance are resolved in `content/issues/done/relay-authoring-preview-experience.md`. A bounded direct continuity comparison supports a narrow participant instruction to preserve earlier source facts while allowing requested invention. Structural acceptance still does not prove that every answer is faithful or that a selected group contains enough distinct cases.

A bounded fresh-host rehearsal distinguishes missing evidence from unreadable output: a middle contribution can satisfy its prompt yet omit concrete source details needed later. The tested literature prompt now asks comparisons to retain their supporting textual evidence. Creative detail retention remains inconsistent in the bounded examples. Shared hosting help explains how to inspect selected material and pause or adapt when it is insufficient; it does not restore facts absent from submitted contributions.

## Unresolved decision

Decide which completeness failures should cause bounded repair, which can safely retain partial useful output, and how those choices interact with changing live walls. Sorting reads against a wall that may have changed during inference, so validating an original request and applying against current state are different checks. Evaluate semantic review separately from deterministic structural validation; a phrase alone does not distinguish an invalid dependency from a valid personal reflection.

## Acceptance condition

Each prompt family has an explicit output-completeness policy with focused reader and application coverage. Repair retains original inputs and the specific failure account. Evaluation reports do not count reader acceptance as proof of task correctness or participant coherence.
