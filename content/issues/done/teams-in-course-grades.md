# Submit and grade team work by team

## Resolution at completion

A team is a section: staff make it, place students in it, and move them, and
the interface says Team wherever a section is shown. An assignment records
whether each student or each section submits it, and the assignment form
offers that choice as Submitted by, asking first when a change on a published
assignment would hide submitted work. On an assignment submitted by each team,
any member submits, every member reads the same attempts with who handed each
in, and the student's assignment page and assignments list show the team, its
members, and where its work stands, with no late-day control. The staff
assignment page lists one row per team with its members named, grades and
delegates per team, sets a due date for the team, and counts what a release
reaches. One assessment is released for the team, and course grades count it
for each current member. A student in no section submits and is graded alone.

After staff change a published assignment to each team, the staff page offers
to move the submissions members handed in on their own, and reports how many
moved to how many teams and how many grades were copied as drafts. A team
whose drafts disagree is flagged before anything is released.

## Decision at completion

There is no Teaming concept and no sets of sections: a team has a section's
course of action, and what differs is what an assignment does with it. One
grade is kept per team, because a copy per member drifts when one is corrected
or retracted; a member who did less than their share is corrected with a
course-grade override that names its reason. Late days are refused on a team
assignment, since a team attempt has no single student to charge. The move
copies grades as drafts for staff to release, so that two members' differing
grades are seen by staff before students. A submission moves once, and only
when it is newer than everything its team has handed in, since a copy takes
the team's next number and would otherwise stand as its latest attempt.
Releasing an assessment sends no notice, to a student or to a team's members,
and nobody is notified when a teammate submits; the pages say who submitted
and when.

## Verification at completion

Application tests form a team, submit one team deliverable, grade and release
it once, and read the same rating on each member's assignment and course
grade; they move earlier submissions with their grades, press the move twice,
and leave an older submission where it is. Browser tests cover the form's
choice, a teammate reading a submitted team attempt, a grader grading a team's
row, and the move with its report.
